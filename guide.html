The Flash-Chilling Science Thermodynamics

Most ready-to-drink bottled coffees rely on 18-hour cold water steeping or high-heat pasteurization. While smooth, cold immersion cannot dissolve the delicate floral esters and bright fruit acids inherent in specialty Arabica beans.

We brew hot at precise pour-over extraction temperatures (92–94°C) to dissolve the complete spectrum of volatile aromatic compounds, and instantly thermal-shock the brew over clean ice directly to 4C. This rapid temperature drop traps volatile flavor molecules before they can escape into the atmosphere.

Processing Style & Freshness Universal Guide

Different single-estate processing methods express unique sensory nuances as they settle in your refrigerator:

🍓 Naturals & Anaerobics Peak: Hours 0–24

High fruit ester concentrations produce explosive wild berry, stone fruit, and winey acidity. Drink within the first 24 hours for maximum aromatic punch.

🌿 Washed Arabica Lots Peak: Hours 12–36

Exhibits supreme cup clarity, clean citrus acidity, and delicate jasmine florality. Highly stable throughout the 48-hour refrigerated window.

🍍 Experimental & Fruit-Fermented Peak: Hours 12–40

Complex enzymatic sugars integrate with chilled aromatics to deliver sparkling green apple and tropical candy sweetness.

Serving & Aeration Protocol Tasting Ritual

Immediate Cold Storage (≤ 4°C)

2
Pour Over High-Density Ice
Pour 150–200ml over solid, clear ice cubes in a short rocks glass. Dense ice chills the beverage without rapid melt-water dilution.
🧊 Clean Melt Density
3
30-Second Aeration Swirl
Gently swirl the glass for 30 seconds. Aeration allows trapped aromatics to bloom as temperature elevates slightly from 4°C to ~8°C.

Freshness FAQs & Best Practices
Why is our shelf life strictly 48 hours? Because we use zero potassium sorbate, sodium benzoate, or thermal pasteurization. Our coffee is live, fresh pour-over designed for peak flavor.
Should I freeze the bottles to store longer? We advise against freezing. Freezing expands water crystal lattices, which breaks down delicate aromatic oils and causes cloudiness upon thawing.
